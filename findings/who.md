# WHO: assessment, correction and accountability

Status: documented chronology with an explicitly stated project assessment.

## The record

The joint team's 2021 ranking is historical. Tedros challenged the laboratory assessment on the day of publication, criticised premature downplaying in July, and WHO explained its position again in August. It would be inaccurate to say WHO never reconsidered. [S02] [S03] [S04] [S05]

SAGO subsequently recommended more investigation. Its later work describes critical gaps in access and verification. WHO's current summary still favours zoonosis on available evidence. [S06] [S07] [S09]

**Responses recorded.** On 30 June 2025, China's National Health Commission said, as reported by the state broadcaster CGTN, that China had shared origins data "without reservation". It described part of the report as raising "groundless requirements for China". [S85] In a reply to the Bundestag dated 30 June 2025, the German government declined to disclose details of the intelligence service's assessment, citing state-interest grounds, and said it is informed by WHO of WHO's findings. We did not find a statement there that the material had been provided to WHO. [S86] We did not find any WHO publication recording that the items in the table were provided after June 2025. That search was bounded: WHO news, speeches and the version 10 risk assessment, together with press reports, checked on 17 November 2026. [S09]

## Project assessment

The confidence of the original ranking exceeded what the limited investigation could justify. That is our assessment of process and communication, supported by the documented limitations and Tedros's contemporaneous objections. It is not a claim that the true origin has now been established.

There is a meaningful difference between issuing a later qualification and explicitly explaining why an earlier assessment carried too much confidence.

## What deserves acknowledgment

WHO should explain:

1. How the joint team's probability language was selected and approved.
2. How unavailable records affected the conclusion, rather than just appearing as a caveat.
3. How the institution handled the disagreement between its director-general and the report.
4. What procedures now prevent a limited investigation from being communicated as a near-exclusion.
5. Whether a prominent correction or contextual note should accompany the historical report.

An apology for overstatement is this project's recommendation. We did not find the direct institutional apology proposed here in the statements reviewed.

## Changes already made

SAGO and the broader origins-investigation framework are substantive institutional developments. The framework addresses laboratory breaches as well as animal pathways. Their existence should be recognised, while implementation and effectiveness remain separate questions. [S10]
