# EcoHealth Alliance, Peter Daszak and NIH

Status: independent audit findings, responses and administrative actions.

## Oversight and reporting

The January 2023 HHS inspector-general audit found that NIH and EcoHealth did not effectively monitor awards and subawards. NIH failed to follow up a late report promptly; EcoHealth had reporting and financial deficiencies and could not obtain requested WIV documentation. The audit identified $89,171 in unallowable costs. [S13]

NIH concurred or generally concurred with the recommendations. EcoHealth disputed important points concerning report timing and interpretation of experimental results. The audit did not independently adjudicate the experimental science; it reported NIH's evaluation and oversight consequences. It also criticised NIH's improper termination of a grant. [S13]

This is a firmer foundation for criticism than treating partisan characterisations of research as settled scientific findings.

## Implementation of the audit recommendations

The audit made 16 recommendations: nine to NIH and seven concerning EcoHealth. OIG's public recommendation tracker, last updated 16 November 2026 and checked on 17 November 2026, lists NIH as the responsible agency for all 16, including those addressed to EcoHealth, and records NIH's response to each as "Concur". It shows 15 as "Closed Implemented" and one as "Open Unimplemented". [S80]

The open recommendation is the one NIH undertook to resolve fastest. In its written comments in the audit, NIH said that within 90 days of publication it would issue a Guide Notice and revise the NIH Grants Policy Statement to define the process and timeline for "immediate notification". The tracker shows that, almost four years after the audit, OIG still treats this as unimplemented, and that the update NIH was expected to give by April 2025 has not been recorded. [S81] [S80] A bounded search of NIH Guide notices on 17 November 2026, including the notices publishing the April 2024 and March 2026 revisions of the Grants Policy Statement, did not find a definition of "immediate notification" for these conditions. That search was not exhaustive.

**Limits:** "Closed Implemented" is OIG's classification of action reported by the agency. The tracker does not publish the evidence behind each closure, and we have not seen it. The audit report records NIH as having "generally concurred" with recommendation 2, while the tracker lists "Concur". The EcoHealth recommendations were closed in October and November 2023, before the suspension and debarment actions described below; the tracker does not say how they relate.

## Subsequent actions

In May 2024, Democratic committee staff also raised concerns about possible misrepresentations, while rejecting claims that the reviewed evidence demonstrated EcoHealth caused the pandemic. That is an attributed political report, not an independent judicial finding. [S27]

HHS issued five-year debarment notices for EcoHealth and Daszak in January 2025. Including the preceding suspension, the notices specify ineligibility through 14 May 2029 and 20 May 2029 respectively. These are historical administrative decisions; any later appeal or modification must be checked before describing current legal status. A bounded web search on 17 November 2026 found no reported appeal or modification, but the official SAM.gov exclusion records could not be checked. [S15] [S16]

## Project recommendations

EcoHealth should provide a point-by-point account of upheld findings and disputed findings, with supporting records. It should explain how access to subrecipient records was enforced and what happened when access failed.

NIH should publish the guidance on "immediate notification" that it committed in January 2023 to issue within 90 days, or explain the delay. That recommendation is still open in OIG's tracker. NIH should also show how the closed recommendations were implemented, demonstrate timely monitoring and clarify escalation rules for unexpected experimental outcomes. [S80] [S81] NIAID's responsible grant-oversight functions belong in that review; this record does not automatically establish personal misconduct by every agency official.

Debarment, reporting failures, hazardous research, viral engineering and causing an outbreak are distinct propositions. Evidence for one must not silently substitute for evidence of another.
