# Journals and Proximal Origin authors

Status: documented publications and disclosures; editorial recommendations remain project judgments.

## The Lancet

In June 2021, The Lancet published an addendum expanding Peter Daszak's competing-interest disclosure for origins-related contributions. It described EcoHealth's relevant work and relationships in China. [S18]

**Project assessment:** readers needed the relevant relationships when evaluating the original intervention. The later disclosure is a correction that should be acknowledged, alongside questions about why the initial process was insufficient.

**The journal's explanation.** The addendum itself explained only that "There may be differences in opinion as to what constitutes a competing interest", and that the journal had invited the 27 authors to re-evaluate their disclosures (as quoted by Times Higher Education). [S92] Richard Horton, the editor-in-chief, gave fuller evidence to the House of Commons Science and Technology Committee on 15 December 2021. He said the journal takes declarations "on trust" and does not investigate every author. It had taken "over a year" to persuade Daszak to make a full declaration, and the June 2021 information "should definitely have been included" in the February 2020 letter. Asked whether methods had changed, he said only that the journal is "now more vigilant" in the context of COVID. [S91] We did not find a published Lancet policy or editorial describing a changed procedure for reviewing disclosures. The search was bounded to the journal's addendum, the committee transcript and press coverage, checked on 17 November 2026.

Ask the journal to explain how disclosures are now reviewed when authors have institutional, research or funding relationships relevant to the subject. A disclosure problem does not itself invalidate every scientific claim in a publication.

## Nature Medicine and Proximal Origin

The 2020 paper argued against laboratory-based scenarios while acknowledging limits on proving or disproving some origins explanations. [S01]

**Project assessment:** the breadth of that dismissal warrants scrutiny of the reasoning, review process and subsequent clarifications. A genomic argument about engineering must not be treated as an audit excluding every research-related infection.

Request a transparent editorial account of the substantive criticisms and relevant correspondence. Evaluate whether clarification, correction or other action is warranted under the journal's standards. A retraction demand requires stronger, specific grounds than a disagreement about interpretation.

Biosafety Now has publicly requested editorial action. This documents an advocacy demand; it does not establish that its allegations or requested remedy are correct. [S28]

**Editorial response on record.** Biosafety Now reports that Nature Medicine acknowledged receipt of its July 2023 letter on 1 August 2023, with no formal response by October 2025. It also reports that a second request, sent on 14 May 2024, had not been acknowledged by June 2024. These are the requester's accounts. [S28] [S88] Secondary reports say that around 22 July 2023 the journal told The Telegraph (one report names the chief editor as the speaker) that retraction was "not warranted" because the paper was a "point of view", not a research study. We have not read the Telegraph article, so this remains a secondhand attribution. [S89] [S90] On 17 November 2026 the article page still classified the paper as Correspondence and showed no editor's note, correction or expression of concern. Crossref listed no update records for its DOI. [S01] [S87] We did not find an editorial, editor's note or formal published reply to the criticisms or correspondence. The search was bounded to the article page, Crossref and web searches, and did not cover Springer Nature's private correspondence.

## Authors' private communications

Early doubts and later published conclusions may reflect learning; they may also raise legitimate questions about certainty or disclosure. The dates, full context, evidence available at each point and authors' responses matter.

Public's July 2023 article reports Andersen's rejection of its interpretation. This initial project has not adjudicated all disputed quotations or reviewed every message. It records the [release provenance](../research/slack-release-provenance.md) and treats a full comparison as open work. [S21]

Before alleging deception, identify the exact public claim, a genuinely inconsistent private statement at the relevant time, the surrounding exchange and the explanation offered.
