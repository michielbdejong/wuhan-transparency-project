# Grading findings, corrective action and follow-up

Status: draft, 17 November 2026 (revised the same day: observation split into positive and concern, boundary test, rules 7 and 8). Applies to findings recorded against the [disclosure controls](controls.md).

## What is graded

A finding records how one actor performed against one control, on stated evidence, at a stated date. It is graded only if the control applies or partly applies to that actor type in the [statement of applicability](applicability.csv). Where a control only partly applies, the finding should say which part was assessed.

A finding grades the disclosure process. It does not grade the actor's scientific conclusions, motives or the cause of the event. A nonconformity about records, for example, does not show what the records would contain.

## Grades

| Grade | Meaning | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Major nonconformity** | Evidence shows the control was absent or failed in a way that prevented independent evaluation of material evidence, or the failure was repeated, systemic or persisted after a specific request. | An independent audit, an administrative or judicial determination, the actor's own record, or a documented statement by a body with a remit to make the request. A project assessment alone is not enough. |
| **Minor nonconformity** | Evidence shows a partial or isolated failure that did not by itself prevent evaluation, or a failure the actor later corrected. | As for a major nonconformity, or a published project assessment whose reasoning and sources are stated. |
| **Opportunity for improvement** | Every clause of the control is shown to be met, but a specific change would make it more effective (see the boundary test below). | A documented source showing the clauses met, and a specific, achievable change. |
| **Observation of concern** | Not a nonconformity, but the evidence shows a weakness that could become one, or the record is ambiguous in a way that matters to the control. Also the only critical grade available for volunteer communities and individuals (rule 7). | A documented source for the weakness or ambiguity. |
| **Positive observation** | The evidence shows the control operating, in whole or in part. | A documented source. Status "control operating" or "control partly operating". |
| **Not evidenced** | We could not see whether the control operated. This is not a negative finding. | A statement of what was looked for, where, and what would settle the question. |

### Boundary between minor nonconformity and opportunity for improvement

Apply this test, clause by clause, to the control statement as written (each "the actor does X").

1. **Is a clause shown unmet?** If a source, not merely our failure to find something, shows that at the time of the conduct the actor did not do something a clause requires, the grade is a nonconformity (minor unless the major criteria are met). The corrective action can then be written as "do what the clause says", without adding to the control.
2. **Are all clauses shown met?** If every clause is shown met, even minimally, and the proposed change asks for more than the wording requires (more prominence than "same prominence", faster than a stated period, more detail than "itemised"), the grade is an opportunity for improvement.
3. **Cannot tell?** If we cannot see whether a clause was met, the grade is "not evidenced", whatever we suspect.

A short check: if the actor, reading only the control statement, could not point to a clause it met, the finding is a nonconformity; if it could point to every clause, the finding is at most an opportunity for improvement.

### Rules for applying grades

1. **Distinguish what we could not see from what failed.** If the only basis for a grade is that we did not find something, the grade is "not evidenced". Use "we did not find" for bounded searches, as the [method](../docs/methodology.md) requires.
2. **Grade at the time of the conduct; record correction in the status.** A disclosure later corrected remains a nonconformity at the time it occurred; the status records "corrected" with the date.
3. **One control per finding.** Where conduct engages several controls, record the principal one and cross-refer.
4. **Attribute.** Grades are this project's assessments. Name the body that made any underlying finding (an inspector general, an advisory group, a committee) and give its remit and limits.
5. **Apply controls even-handedly.** The same control is applied in the same way to every actor type it covers, including bodies whose releases or reports serve as evidence elsewhere in the project.
6. **Record positive observations.** Where evidence shows a control operating, record it. A catalogue that records only failures misdescribes the process audited.
7. **Volunteer communities and individuals receive observations only.** Volunteer-edited reference works (such as Wikipedia articles) and individual authors, acting as individuals rather than as officers of an organisation, are not graded with nonconformities, opportunities for improvement or "not evidenced". They receive positive observations or observations of concern; where we cannot see what happened, an observation of concern is recorded only if a documented source shows the ambiguity, and otherwise nothing is graded. No corrective-action request is addressed to them, because there is no addressee with management authority and requests to individuals tend to become pressure on people. Where a change would help, the finding records a **suggestion**, to be raised, if at all, through the community's own processes (a talk-page proposal or edit request, a journal's correspondence route) in the proposer's own words, with any conflict of interest disclosed. Institutional duties around them are graded against the institution that holds them, for example the journal that published an author's disclosure. Where an individual acts for an organisation, the organisation is graded.
8. **Do not judge the project's own requests.** Where the request at issue under D.5.5 was made by this observatory, non-response is graded no higher than an observation of concern.

## Corrective-action requests

A finding graded as a nonconformity, an observation of concern, an opportunity for improvement or "not evidenced" may carry a corrective-action request, except a finding about a volunteer community or individual, which carries at most a suggestion (rule 7). A positive observation carries a request only for a part of the control not shown operating. A request should:

- name the actor and the control;
- state a specific, achievable action (for "not evidenced", usually: publish or produce the evidence that would show the control operating);
- avoid requiring the actor to adopt this project's conclusions or wording;
- be achievable within the actor's own powers (an international body cannot be asked to compel a member state).

Because the observatory has no authority over the actor, a request is a published request. Where the actor has a public contact route for corrections or enquiries, the request may be sent through it, and the date recorded. Requests are not to be directed at individual volunteer editors or organised as pressure campaigns.

## Follow-up (surveillance) checks

Each open finding has a follow-up date. Defaults: three months for dynamic sources such as reference articles; six months for institutional requests; twelve months where no response is realistically expected. At follow-up, recheck the public record and update the status.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| open | Corrective action requested; no adequate response yet. |
| evidence requested | Graded "not evidenced"; evidence that would settle the question has been requested or sought. |
| response received | The actor responded; the response is recorded and under review. |
| corrected | The actor took corrective action; date and source recorded. |
| rebutted | The actor's reasoned response, or new evidence, shows the finding was wrong or overstated; the finding is regraded or withdrawn and the change recorded. |
| control operating / control partly operating | Positive observation; no corrective action outstanding (or only for the part noted). |
| suggestion recorded | Observation about a volunteer community or individual; a suggestion is recorded for the community's own processes and no corrective-action request is outstanding. The record is still rechecked at the follow-up date. |
| closed | No further follow-up planned, with the reason stated. |

An unanswered request stays open; silence is recorded as "no response found by" the follow-up date, not as an admission. A full re-review of every finding in a case should take place at least annually, alongside the surveillance checks.

## Changes to grades

Regrading, withdrawal and closure follow the project's corrections practice: record the change, the date, the reason and the source in the case file and in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).
