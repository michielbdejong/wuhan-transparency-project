# Case: COVID-19 origins — disclosure findings

Status: first regrading, 17 November 2026; revised the same day after review (see [regrading log](#regrading-log)). Findings: [findings.csv](findings.csv). Controls: [framework/controls.md](../../framework/controls.md). Grades: [framework/grading.md](../../framework/grading.md).

## What this regrading is

The project's existing dossiers, proposals and evidence register have been re-read and their disclosure-related material restated as findings against the [disclosure controls](../../framework/controls.md). No new research was done. Every finding cites source IDs already in the [source register](../../sources/register.json) and traces to text in [findings/](../../findings/), [proposals/](../../proposals/) or [research/](../../research/), or to the access notes in the register itself. Where the existing record does not support a grade, the finding is graded "not evidenced" (for individuals and volunteer communities, see grading rule 7).

The findings concern disclosure processes only: records, data access, reporting, interests, communication of confidence, correction and release provenance. They do not assess the origin of SARS-CoV-2 or the science of any research. Where a finding touches research conduct, it is described only at the level the source register already uses, such as "a grant-compliance question". The project's existing limits apply: see [what this project does not establish](../../README.md#what-this-project-does-not-establish).

Grades are this project's assessments, applying [grading rules](../../framework/grading.md) that require an independent audit, an administrative determination or a documented statement by a body with a remit before a major nonconformity is recorded. The underlying audit and administrative findings belong to the bodies named in the sources.

## Did the framework work?

It fitted best where an independent body had already examined a records or reporting duty: the HHS inspector general's audit, the WIV determination and SAGO's account of refused requests translate directly into controls, grades, responses and corrective actions, and the cycle made it natural to record NIH's concurrence and the inspector general's own conduct as positive observations alongside the failures.

### Where the framework strains

- **Individuals and volunteer communities.** Rule 7 removes the problem of a corrective-action request with no addressee, but at a cost: a sixteen-month lag in a widely read reference article (F16) and an incomplete initial disclosure (F12) now carry the same grade as an ambiguous record (F15). Severity for these actors is carried by the finding text, not the grade, and accountability shifts to institutions such as journals (F13, F14). Wikipedia has no institution with editorial authority over content, so for it the shift has nowhere to go.
- **Seeing from outside.** The boundary test made the minor-nonconformity/improvement line checkable, but it also showed how rarely an outside auditor can confirm that every clause is met: no opportunity for improvement survives in this case, and F03 moved to "not evidenced". The same limit governs F21, where the official exclusion register could not be fetched. Four findings now wait on evidence that only the actors, or access the project lacks, can supply.
- **Response to the observatory itself.** D.5.5 cannot be applied until the project sends dated requests through actors' published routes, and rule 8 caps any finding on its own requests at an observation of concern, so the control will mostly yield observations here.
- **Audit bodies graded as actors.** The new audit-body column makes it possible to grade the inspector general consistently, but D.5.3 fits it only in part (F11): who audits the auditor is outside what public sources show.
- **Unresolved gaps.** Candidate findings on WHO's documentation of its headline ranking (D.4.4) and on a register of outstanding evidence requests (D.1.3) remain folded into the corrective actions of F01 and F04. The statement of applicability now has columns for news publishers and audit bodies; executive agencies issuing determinations are treated as national government authorities rather than given their own column.
