# Case: COVID-19 origins — disclosure findings

Status: first regrading, 17 November 2026. Findings: [findings.csv](findings.csv). Controls: [framework/controls.md](../../framework/controls.md). Grades: [framework/grading.md](../../framework/grading.md).

## What this regrading is

The project's existing dossiers, proposals and evidence register have been re-read and their disclosure-related material restated as findings against the [disclosure controls](../../framework/controls.md). No new research was done. Every finding cites source IDs already in the [source register](../../sources/register.json) and traces to text in [findings/](../../findings/), [proposals/](../../proposals/) or [research/](../../research/). Where the existing record does not support a grade, the finding is graded "not evidenced".

The findings concern disclosure processes only: records, data access, reporting, interests, communication of confidence, correction and release provenance. They do not assess the origin of SARS-CoV-2 or the science of any research. Where a finding touches research conduct, it is described only at the level the source register already uses, such as "a grant-compliance question". The project's existing limits apply: see [what this project does not establish](../../README.md#what-this-project-does-not-establish).

Grades are this project's assessments, applying [grading rules](../../framework/grading.md) that require an independent audit, an administrative determination or a documented statement by a body with a remit before a major nonconformity is recorded. The underlying audit and administrative findings belong to the bodies named in the sources.

## Did the framework work?

It fitted best where an independent body had already examined a records or reporting duty: the HHS inspector general's audit, the WIV determination and SAGO's account of refused requests translate directly into controls, grades, responses and corrective actions, and the cycle made it natural to record NIH's concurrence and the inspector general's own conduct as positive observations alongside the failures. It strained where the actor is an individual or a volunteer community: a corrective-action request to "English Wikipedia" has no addressee with management authority, and grading authors' confidence (F15) depends on a full comparison of public and private statements that the project has not done, so the framework correctly yields "not evidenced" but says little. It also strained on severity: the line between a minor nonconformity and an opportunity for improvement for WHO's 2021 ranking or for Wikipedia's currency is a judgment the evidence does not fix, and the "observation" grade has to carry both praise and concern. Three gaps stand out: there is no column in the statement of applicability for news publishers, although Public is graded here; there is no control requiring official determinations to be published by the issuing body (the HHS debarment documents cited here were found only on committee websites, S14–S16); and there is no control covering an actor's response to an outside observatory itself, which is distinct from responding to criticism in its own field. Candidate findings on WHO's documentation of its headline ranking (D.4.4) and on a register of outstanding evidence requests (D.1.3) were folded into the corrective actions of F01 and F04 rather than recorded separately.
