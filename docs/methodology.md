# Method and editorial standards

Reviewed: 14 November 2026.

## Scope

This project examines the handling of evidence, research oversight and communication about COVID-19 origins. It is not an exhaustive evaluation of evolutionary, epidemiological or intelligence evidence.

Use these distinctions consistently:

- **Animal spillover**: infection acquired through animals outside a research-related pathway.
- **Research-related origin**: an infection connected with field research or laboratory activity; this need not involve an engineered virus.
- **Engineered virus**: a claim about how a virus was produced or modified, distinct from how a person became infected.
- **Transparency failure**: a failure to disclose or permit verification; it does not identify what undisclosed records would show.
- **Administrative debarment**: an eligibility decision about government business, distinct from a criminal conviction or a finding of pandemic causation.

## Claim categories

| Category | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Documented event or statement | A source establishes that an event happened or a statement was made. |
| Audit or administrative finding | A named authority made the finding; its remit, responses and limitations matter. |
| Disputed allegation | A claim remains contested or insufficiently established. |
| Project assessment | Our evaluation of evidence, wording or institutional practice. |
| Proposal | Wording or action advocated by this project. |
| Open question | Evidence needed to reach a conclusion remains missing. |

A source can be excellent evidence of a publication date and poor evidence of the motives it alleges. Committee announcements and advocacy letters are labelled accordingly. Independent audit findings receive more weight for compliance questions than partisan summaries.

## Verification and limits

The initial dossier was drafted with AI assistance from public-source research. Relevant passages were reviewed, but not every linked document was read in full. The source register records access limits, including indexed text used when publisher pages were blocked.

Dates distinguish the event, publication, later republication and this project's review. Wikipedia observations refer to retrieved versions and, where available, permanent revision links. They do not establish an editor's intent.

No full comparison of the 2023 and 2026 Slack collections has been completed. No claim about their complete overlap should be inferred.

## Corrections and responses

Record an organisation's own response, later correction and genuine remedial work. A change in scientific assessment can be appropriate; the accountability question is whether the earlier confidence was justified and accurately communicated.

Use "we did not find" for bounded searches. Avoid universal claims that an organisation never acknowledged an issue unless an adequate review establishes that.

Retraction requires grounds evaluated under journal standards. Private uncertainty, a later change of view, a conflict of interest, a reporting violation and research fraud are different issues.

## Citation and reuse

Source IDs resolve through the annotated source register. Link to originals, keep quotations short, and preserve context. Linked publications and any quoted passages retain their original copyright and licensing terms. This repository does not redistribute the underlying Slack/email collections.

For Wikipedia, cite independent reliable publications and relevant primary statements directly. This project is not a substitute for those sources. Follow the site's sourcing, neutrality and discussion procedures; do not organise pressure on individual editors.
