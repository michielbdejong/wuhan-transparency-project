# Questions institutions should answer

These are research and accountability questions, not allegations embedded as facts.

| Institution | Question | Useful response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WHO | How was the 2021 probability ranking approved despite limited access? | Decision process, evidence standard and treatment of dissent |
| WHO | What changed after leadership challenged the assessment? | Implemented procedures and evaluation of their effectiveness |
| Chinese authorities | Which requested early-case, animal and laboratory data remain unavailable, and why? | An itemised inventory, reasons and release timetable |
| WIV | Why were the records requested by NIH not provided? | Original records, correspondence and an independently verifiable explanation |
| EcoHealth | How did it enforce access and reporting obligations for subrecipients? | Agreements, monitoring records and responses to audit findings |
| NIH/NIAID | Why did monitoring fail to trigger timely follow-up? | Remediation status, escalation rules and evidence of compliance |
| The Lancet | Why were relevant relationships not fully disclosed initially? | Editorial review of the disclosure process and resulting changes |
| Nature Medicine | How has it evaluated substantive criticisms and correspondence relating to Proximal Origin? | A reasoned editorial response addressing evidence and uncertainty |
| Proximal Origin authors | How did their assessments change over time, and which evidence drove those changes? | A contextual chronology linked to complete relevant exchanges |
| Wikipedia editors | Can the current assessment and missing-data limitations be represented more clearly? | Source-based article improvements through ordinary editorial discussion |

A satisfactory response may rebut a criticism. Record it and revise the project accordingly. The goal is an accurate public record and better institutional practice.
