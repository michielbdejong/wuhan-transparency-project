# Proposed Wikipedia edits

Status: drafts only. No Wikipedia edits or talk-page posts have been made by this project. Drafted 14 November 2026; rechecked against the live articles on 17 November 2026.

Check the current article and talk-page discussion before use. The [observations](../findings/wikipedia.md) describe the versions reviewed and record which revisions were checked.

## Before using these drafts

**Contentious topic.** On English Wikipedia, COVID-19 is designated by the Arbitration Committee as a [contentious topic][S71] (shortcut WP:CT/COVID). The designation replaced the community's COVID-19 general sanctions in June 2021 and applies to related pages and parts of pages, including the articles below. [S71] [S72] Within a contentious topic, editors must edit carefully and constructively, comply with all applicable policies and guidelines, follow editorial and behavioural best practice, respect any page restrictions and err on the side of caution. Uninvolved administrators may impose editor restrictions (including blocks, topic bans and revert restrictions) and page restrictions (including protection, revert limits and "consensus required"). [S70] A June 2026 amendment replaced the formal awareness requirement with warnings; administrators are reminded that warning before sanctioning is almost always best practice, but no one should rely on receiving a formal alert first. [S71]

**Page restrictions.** At the 17 November 2026 check, COVID-19 lab leak theory and Origin of SARS-CoV-2 were both under extended-confirmed protection, so newer accounts cannot edit them directly and must use edit requests on the talk page. The talk page of the lab-leak article carries a contentious-topic page-restriction notice. [S75] [S76] [S77] Check the current protection and editnotices before acting, as they can change.

**Starting points, not finished text.** The wording below is a starting point. Anyone who proposes a change should rewrite it in their own words, read the sources themselves and cite independent reliable sources directly. Do not cite this repository, link to it as support, or present its assessments as established.

**Propose on the talk page.** We recommend proposing changes on the article talk page and waiting for discussion rather than editing the articles directly. Read the talk-page header, any current-consensus notice and recent threads first. A thread opened on 8 October 2026 on the lab-leak article's talk page ("Lead states more than the sources establish in a few places") already proposes adding the 2025 SAGO assessment and related lead changes; it had no replies in the revision checked. Contributing to an existing discussion is better than opening a duplicate. [S75]

**Conflict of interest and advocacy.** This project advocates specific changes to these articles, so affiliation with it is an external relationship that may create a conflict of interest. Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest guideline says editors with a COI should disclose it, are strongly discouraged from editing affected articles directly and can propose changes on talk pages instead, for example with the edit COI template. [S73] Wikipedia is also not a venue for promoting an organisation's agenda. [S74] Project contributors should disclose the affiliation when proposing changes, leave decisions to uninvolved editors, and not coordinate others to support a proposal.

## 1. Add the current assessment to Origin of SARS-CoV-2

Suggested location: the WHO subsection, after its account of the 2022 preliminary report.

> In June 2025, WHO's Scientific Advisory Group for the Origins of Novel Pathogens concluded that available evidence favoured zoonotic spillover. It could not adequately assess a laboratory-related origin because essential information about research activities and biosafety conditions in Wuhan laboratories had not been provided, and therefore could not exclude that possibility.

Cite [JAMA's independent summary][S23] and [WHO's announcement][S08]. Use the same information, shortened as appropriate, in the lab-leak article's lead if editorial discussion supports its prominence.

This retains the assessment favouring zoonosis. It does not imply equal probabilities or an institutional finding of a cover-up.

## 2. Clarify attribution in the Proximal Origin article

Candidate replacement for the sentence describing what the paper firmly determined:

> The authors concluded that SARS-CoV-2 was not a laboratory construct or a purposefully manipulated virus.

The original paper supports attribution of this conclusion. [S01] Retain appropriate secondary sourcing and surrounding discussion. The purpose is to distinguish the authors' conclusion from an unqualified encyclopedic assertion.

**Status at 17 November 2026: not yet addressed.** [Revision 1367675190][S26] (4 August 2026), the version reviewed on 14 November, is still the current revision. It says the paper was the first scientific report to "firmly determine" its conclusion; the phrase is quoted from a secondary headline but the sentence itself is in Wikipedia's voice. A search of the article's talk page, as retrieved on 17 November 2026, found no discussion of this wording.

## 3. Discussion proposal

A talk-page proposal might cover the points below. Rewrite it in your own words, cite the sources you have read, disclose any affiliation with this project, and check first whether an existing thread (such as the 8 October 2026 thread noted above) already covers the point.

> Could the article's opening and WHO section more clearly summarise the June 2025 SAGO assessment? JAMA reports that it favoured zoonotic spillover but could not exclude a laboratory incident because requested information was unavailable. The article already covers earlier WHO objections; the proposed addition would make the current assessment and its limitations easier to find.

## 4. Say why evidence is missing (COVID-19 misinformation, origin section)

Current wording: "An alternative hypothesis under investigation, deemed unlikely by the majority of virologists given a lack of evidence, is that the virus may have accidentally escaped from the Wuhan Institute of Virology in the course of standard research." [S98]

Candidate replacement:

> An alternative hypothesis under investigation, which most virologists consider less likely, is that the virus accidentally escaped from the Wuhan Institute of Virology in the course of research. In 2025 WHO's Scientific Advisory Group for the Origins of Novel Pathogens said it could not assess this possibility because requested laboratory records had not been provided.

Cite SAGO's 2025 assessment and an independent summary such as JAMA's. [S07] [S23] The wording keeps the majority view and states the reason the evidence is incomplete, without implying equal probabilities.

## 5. Narrow the conspiracy-theory sentence (COVID-19 lab leak theory, lead)

Current wording: "Many scenarios proposed for a lab leak are characteristic of conspiracy theories." [S76]

Candidate replacement: say which scenarios, with their sources. For example, claims of deliberate release or bioweapon development have the characteristics of conspiracy theories, while an accidental infection during research is a hypothesis that investigators, including SAGO, have said cannot be assessed or excluded on the information provided. After "There is no evidence supporting laboratory involvement…", add that WHO's advisory group reported that the records needed to assess it were not provided. [S07]

## 6. Separate the accident scenario in the misinformation lead

Current wording lists "a bioweapon accidentally or deliberately leaked from a laboratory" among unevidenced claims. [S98] Candidate: keep the bioweapon claim in that list, and add a clause pointing to the origin section, where an accidental escape is described as a hypothesis under investigation.

## 7. Add how the misinformation label was applied (COVID-19 misinformation)

Suggested location: the lab-leak passage, near the existing sentences on journalists and media softening their language. Draft:

> In February 2020, 27 scientists published a statement in The Lancet condemning "conspiracy theories suggesting that COVID-19 does not have a natural origin". Emails later obtained through public-records requests showed that it was organised by Peter Daszak of EcoHealth Alliance, which had funded coronavirus research at the Wuhan Institute of Virology; in June 2021 The Lancet published an addendum expanding his competing-interest disclosure. From February to May 2021, Facebook removed posts claiming that the virus was man-made, before reversing the policy in light of ongoing investigations.

Cite the statement, the addendum and secondary reporting directly: The Lancet [S96] [S18], Times Higher Education [S92] and AP [S99]. Cite US Right to Know [S97] only together with secondary coverage, and attribute it as an advocacy group. If critics' view that this amounted to steering the debate is included, attribute it to named critics in reliable sources and include the responses of those concerned.

## Editorial boundaries

Use reliable published sources directly. Do not cite this repository as independent authority for its own judgments, present the project's assessment of WHO's 2021 confidence or its [acknowledgment criteria](who-accountability-requests.md) as fact, or infer misconduct from disagreement. Review source context and Wikipedia's applicable editorial requirements before submitting.
