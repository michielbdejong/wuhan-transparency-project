## 1. Add the current assessment to Origin of SARS-CoV-2

Suggested location: the WHO subsection, after its account of the 2022 preliminary report.

> In June 2025, WHO's Scientific Advisory Group for the Origins of Novel Pathogens concluded that available evidence favoured zoonotic spillover. It could not adequately assess a laboratory-related origin because essential information about research activities and biosafety conditions in Wuhan laboratories had not been provided, and therefore could not exclude that possibility.

Cite [JAMA's independent summary][S23] and [WHO's announcement][S08]. Use the same information, shortened as appropriate, in the lab-leak article's lead if editorial discussion supports its prominence.

## 2. Clarify attribution in the Proximal Origin article

Candidate replacement for the sentence describing what the paper firmly determined:

> The authors concluded that SARS-CoV-2 was not a laboratory construct or a purposefully manipulated virus.

The original paper supports attribution of this conclusion. [S01] Retain appropriate secondary sourcing and surrounding discussion. The purpose is to distinguish the authors' conclusion from an unqualified encyclopedic assertion.

## 3. Discussion proposal

A concise talk-page proposal could say:

> Could the article's opening and WHO section more clearly summarise the June 2025 SAGO assessment? JAMA reports that it favoured zoonotic spillover but could not exclude a laboratory incident because requested information was unavailable. The article already covers earlier WHO objections; the proposed addition would make the current assessment and its limitations easier to find.
