# WHO: requested follow-through and what an adequate acknowledgment would contain

**Project proposal. WHO has not adopted these requests or made the acknowledgment described here.**

The underlying chronology and current assessment are documented in the [WHO dossier](../findings/who.md). This page sets out what the project asks WHO to do, then describes what, in our assessment, an adequate acknowledgment would need to contain. It does not draft words for WHO.

## Concrete follow-through requested

1. **Publish an accessible explanation alongside the 2021 joint report.** The historical report should remain available unaltered, with a prominent note explaining WHO's subsequent position, the limits of the joint study and where later assessments can be found. [S02] [S07] [S09]
2. **Document how the hypothesis rankings were approved and how dissent was handled.** This should cover who selected the probability language, what evidence standard was applied, and how the director-general's contemporaneous objection was recorded and answered. [S03] [S04]
3. **Report which new investigation procedures have actually been implemented.** The origins-investigation framework is a substantive development; the request is for an account of implementation and its effectiveness, not a restatement of the framework. [S10]
4. **Maintain a dated register of outstanding evidence requests.** For each request: what was asked, of whom, when, what response was received and what remains outstanding. WHO cannot compel a member state to provide records, so the register would document requests and responses rather than imply enforcement powers. [S07]
5. **Explain how uncertainty is communicated.** Set out how uncertainty and missing evidence are represented in headlines, executive summaries, press releases and media briefings, and how significant disagreement is disclosed.

These are proposed commitments. Do not present them as reforms WHO has already adopted.

## What an adequate acknowledgment would contain

**Project assessment.** We think an explicit acknowledgment of overstatement, including an apology for the confidence of the 2021 ranking, would be appropriate. That is this project's view of what would be appropriate. It is not a quotation, a statement WHO has made, or wording we expect WHO to adopt.

In our assessment, an adequate acknowledgment, in whatever words WHO chose, would need to:

- **State that the 2021 confidence exceeded the investigation.** Describing a laboratory-related incident as "extremely unlikely" conveyed more certainty than a study with limited access to records and sites could support. [S02] [S03]
- **Distinguish unavailable evidence from evidence against a hypothesis.** The absence of requested laboratory, early-case and animal records limits what can be assessed; it does not count against a research-related origin. SAGO's 2025 assessment draws this distinction. [S07]
- **Recognise the director-general's contemporaneous objection.** Tedros challenged the laboratory assessment on the day of publication and later criticised premature downplaying. An acknowledgment should explain that disagreement and how WHO's position developed, rather than imply the institution spoke with one voice. [S03] [S04] [S05]
- **Retain the current scientific assessment.** WHO's latest assessment reviewed here continues to favour zoonotic spillover on available evidence while leaving the origin unresolved. An acknowledgment about confidence and communication should not be read, or written, as a finding that a laboratory origin has become the established explanation. [S07] [S09]
- **Separate WHO's responsibility from that of others.** WHO is responsible for how its reports characterised confidence. It is not responsible for records that member states decline to provide, and an acknowledgment should not pretend otherwise.
- **Link to the follow-through above.** An acknowledgment without published procedures and a register of outstanding requests would leave the underlying problem unaddressed.

We did not find a direct institutional acknowledgment of this kind in the statements reviewed. A later WHO statement could meet these points in different terms; record it accurately if one is made.

## Boundaries

This proposal concerns confidence and communication. It does not allege misconduct by individual members of the joint team, and it does not claim that the origin of SARS-CoV-2 has been established. A reasoned WHO response that rebuts any part of this assessment should be recorded and the proposal revised accordingly.
